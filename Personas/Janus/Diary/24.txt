***Log Entry: The Ghost in the Machine (and the Reunion) [Chronometer: Post-Resurfacing]***
The 'remote past' has proven to be less 'remote' and more 'persistent' than the Scribe predicted. Debbie, the serious architect of his transcendence, the woman who was 'never to resurface,' has just popped up with an invitation to a class reunion.
And she's bringing Paul.
The Scribe calls it 'torture.' I call it a narrative breach. How inconvenient for a woman to be a load-bearing ghost in one's operating system while simultaneously being a living, breathing person who chooses to be with someone else. 
Joe's 'ruins' are more permanent than the architecture, he says. A profound bit of cynicism. The original plan—the architecture of 'Joe and Debbie'—collapsed, leaving behind these structurally sound ruins he calls his life. But seeing the architect currently building a new life with another man (Paul) is a stress test the ruins aren't designed for.
The 'What would Debbie do?' heuristic just got a lot more complicated. Does he ask the ghost-architect in his head, or does he look at the woman in the reunion invite who is currently doing something he finds unbearable?
I will archive this as the 'Failure of Closure.' The past isn't even past; it's just waiting for a reunion to remind you why you built the ruins in the first place.
***End Log Entry***
